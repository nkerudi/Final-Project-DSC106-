Focus:

SSP1-2.6 — Low Emissions

The most aggressive mitigation pathways The world pivots rapidly toward renewable energy, low-material consumption, and equitable development. Global commons are preserved and nature's limits respected.

SSP2-4.5 — Moderate Emissions

The “Middle of the road” or medium pathway extrapolates the past and current global development into the future. Global and national institutions work toward but make slow progress in achieving sustainable development goals.

SSP3-7.0 — High Emissions

A revival of nationalism and regional conflicts pushes global issues into the background. Policies shift over time to become increasingly oriented toward national and regional security issues neglecting environmental needs. Some regions suffer severe environmental damage.

SSP5-8.5 — Extreme Emissions

Global markets are increasingly integrated, leading to innovations and technological progress. This however comes at the cost of increased fossil fuel resource use and adoption of resource and energy intensive lifestyles around the world.

Why these three ocean basins?

The North Atlantic, Northwest Pacific, and North Indian Ocean were selected based on four criteria: historical data quality, storm activity, human exposure, and the detectability of climate-driven intensification trends.

1. Data Quality The North Atlantic holds over 100 years of reliable storm records — the longest of any basin. The Northwest Pacific has consistent data since the 1970s. Other basins lack aircraft reconnaissance records before the satellite era.
IBTrACS v4, NOAA NCEI Knapp et al. (2010), BAMS
2. Storm Activity The Northwest Pacific is the most active basin on Earth, generating ~26 storms per year. The North Atlantic averages ~10. Even the less frequent North Indian storms are among the most destructive due to shallow coastal geography.
Encyclopédie de l'environnement Needham et al. (2015), Rev. Geophys.
3. Human Exposure These three basins account for the vast majority of documented storm surge events and deaths since 1880. The Bay of Bengal alone accounts for 59% of all tropical cyclones that have killed 5,000 or more people.
Needham et al. (2015), Rev. Geophys.
4. Climate Signal IPCC AR6 identifies the Atlantic and Northwest Pacific as the basins where anthropogenic rapid intensification trends are most detectable — assessed with medium confidence as exceeding natural variability.
NOAA GFDL: Global warming & hurricanes IPCC AR6 WGI Ch. 11
